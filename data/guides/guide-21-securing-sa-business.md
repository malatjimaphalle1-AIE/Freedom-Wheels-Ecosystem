## Why Business Security Matters More Than Ever

South Africa has seen a surge in:

- **Domain squatting** — someone registers your brand name as a `.co.za` or `.com` before you do.
- **Brand cloning** — copycats scrape your site and resell under a similar name.
- **Phishing on your domain** — attackers send fake invoices from `yourbrand-invoices.com`.
- **Trademark hijacking** — competitors register your unregistered brand and demand you stop using it.

Every one of these is preventable for under R3,000 if you act early. Each can cost R50,000+ to fix later.

## Domain Registration Strategy

Your domain is your digital address. Treat it like property.

### What to Register

When you launch a business, register:

1. **The `.co.za`** — your primary SA domain.
2. **The `.com`** — international protection (essential if you ever expand).
3. **Common misspellings** — if your name is hard to spell, grab the variants.
4. **Defensive TLDs** — `.net`, `.org`, `.africa` if you have budget.

### Domain Privacy Protection

Without privacy protection, your name, email, and phone number are publicly listed in the WHOIS database. Spammers scrape this constantly. Hostinger bundles privacy protection free with most domains — make sure it is enabled.

### Domain Lock

Enable "registrar lock" on every domain. This prevents unauthorised transfers. A surprising number of SA businesses lose their domain because they left this off and an attacker socially-engineered a transfer.

### Renewal Strategy

Set a calendar reminder 30 days before every domain expires. Auto-renew is good, but a manual check is better. Use Hostinger's bulk renewal tool and renew for 3–5 years at a time — discounts often apply.

- Two-factor authentication on your account.

### WordPress Hardening Checklist

If you run WordPress (most SA small businesses do):

1. **Update everything weekly** — WordPress core, themes, plugins.
2. **Delete unused plugins** — every plugin is a potential attack surface.
3. **Use Wordfence or Sucuri** for firewall and malware scanning.
4. **Disable file editing** in `wp-config.php`.
5. **Limit login attempts** to prevent brute force.
6. **Use strong admin passwords** — store in Bitwarden or 1Password.
7. **Change the admin username** from "admin" to something unique.
8. **Force SSL on wp-admin** so credentials are encrypted.

### Backups

Your host should back up daily, but you should also keep an off-host backup. Use UpdraftPlus to send weekly backups to Google Drive or Dropbox. If your host gets compromised, your off-host backup is your recovery point.

## Trademark Basics for SA Businesses

A registered business name with CIPC does NOT give you trademark protection. This is the most common (and most expensive) misconception in SA business.

### What a Trademark Protects

A trademark gives you exclusive rights to use your brand name, logo, slogan, or unique packaging in your industry across South Africa. Without it, anyone can use your brand name — even if you registered the company.

### Where to Register

Register through **CIPC** (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) at `cipc.co.za`. The application fee is R590 per class of goods/services.

### Trademark Classes You Probably Need

- Class 9 — software, downloadable content.
- Class 35 — advertising, business services.
- Class 41 — education, entertainment, content.
- Class 42 — IT services, SaaS.

Pick classes based on what your business actually does. A SA digital agency needs Class 35 and 42. A podcast host needs Class 41.

### Trademark Process

1. **Search** the CIPC database for conflicts (free).
2. **Apply** with a description of your goods/services.
3. **Wait** — approval typically takes 6–12 months.
4. **Renew** every 10 years.

### International Protection

If you sell outside SA, file a Madrid Protocol international application. Cost: R15,000 — R30,000 depending on countries covered.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Registering the company but not the trademark.** Two different protections.
2. **Letting domains auto-renew without checking.** Card expires, you lose the domain.
3. **Using a free SSL that requires manual renewal.** Use Let's Encrypt auto-renewal.
4. **Ignoring plugin updates.** Outdated plugins are the #1 WordPress hack vector.
5. **No backups off-host.** When your host gets hacked, your backup dies with it.
6. **Skipping trademark filing "until you grow."** The bigger you grow, the more attractive a target you are.
